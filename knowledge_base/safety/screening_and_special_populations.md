# Health Screening and Special Populations

Source: Coachin summary of pre-participation screening practice (PAR-Q+ style questions; ACSM preparticipation screening recommendations) and widely published guidance for specific groups. General guidance; individuals should follow their own clinician's advice.

## Screening questions before training

Medical clearance from a doctor is recommended before starting or intensifying exercise if the person answers yes to any of the following:

- Has a doctor ever said you have a heart condition or high blood pressure that is not controlled?
- Do you feel pain in your chest at rest, during daily activities or when you exercise?
- Do you lose balance because of dizziness, or have you lost consciousness in the last 12 months?
- Have you been diagnosed with another chronic condition (other than heart disease or high blood pressure) such as diabetes, kidney disease or lung disease?
- Are you currently taking prescribed medications for a chronic condition?
- Do you currently have (or have had in the last 12 months) a bone, joint or soft tissue problem that could be made worse by becoming more physically active?
- Has a doctor ever said you should only do medically supervised physical activity?
- Are you pregnant or have you given birth in the last 6 months?

Until cleared, keep exercise light to moderate (RPE 7 or below), avoid maximal efforts and high-intensity intervals, and stop if any symptoms appear.

## High blood pressure

- Regular aerobic and resistance exercise helps lower blood pressure over time.
- Uncontrolled or very high blood pressure needs medical clearance before training.
- Avoid holding the breath while lifting (Valsalva manoeuvre); breathe out during the effort.
- Prefer moderate loads and higher reps over maximal lifts; be careful with long isometric holds and head-down positions.

## Diabetes

- Exercise improves blood sugar control. People using insulin or medications that can cause low blood sugar should check glucose before, during (for long sessions) and after exercise, and carry fast-acting carbohydrate.
- Check feet regularly if there is reduced sensation, and wear appropriate footwear.

## Pregnancy and postpartum

- For uncomplicated pregnancies, regular moderate activity is generally recommended, with approval from the person's obstetric provider.
- Avoid contact sports, activities with a high risk of falling, exercising in excessive heat, and lying flat on the back for long periods after the first trimester.
- Stop and contact a provider for vaginal bleeding, fluid leakage, regular painful contractions, dizziness, chest pain, calf pain or swelling, or decreased fetal movement.
- After birth, return gradually and get clearance (often around 6 weeks, longer after a caesarean section); pelvic floor and abdominal rehabilitation may be needed before high-impact exercise.

## Older adults

- Strength and balance training reduce fall risk and help maintain independence.
- Start with lighter loads and simple movements, progress gradually, and include balance work (e.g. single-leg stands near support) on 3+ days per week.
- People with osteoporosis should avoid loaded spinal flexion and twisting (e.g. sit-ups, heavy Russian twists) and seek guidance on safe loading.

## Young people (under 18)

- Supervised resistance training with good technique is safe and beneficial for adolescents.
- Focus on technique and bodyweight or light loads before heavy maximal lifting.
- Nutrition advice should support growth; do not recommend calorie restriction for minors without medical supervision.

## Asthma

- Warm up gradually, keep reliever medication on hand, and be careful in cold, dry air.
- Stop and use reliever medication for wheeze or chest tightness; seek medical help if it does not resolve.
