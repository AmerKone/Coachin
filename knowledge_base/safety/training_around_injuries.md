# Training Around Pain and Common Injuries

Source: Coachin summary of common physiotherapy practice (pain-monitoring approach used in tendon and joint rehabilitation). This is not diagnosis or treatment: new, severe or worsening pain should be assessed by a doctor or physiotherapist.

## The pain-monitoring approach

For known, already-assessed minor issues (e.g. mild tendon irritation), many clinicians allow exercise within these limits:

- Pain during exercise stays at or below about 3 out of 10.
- Pain settles within about 24 hours after the session.
- Pain and stiffness the next morning are not worse than usual.

If any limit is exceeded, reduce load, range of motion or volume next time. Sharp pain, swelling or pain above 5/10 means stop and get assessed.

## General modification strategies

- Reduce the range of motion to the pain-free portion.
- Slow the tempo (e.g. 3-second lowering) and lighten the load.
- Swap to a variation that loads the area less (examples below).
- Train other body parts normally: an injury in one area rarely means stopping all exercise.
- Avoid high-impact, jumping and explosive movements until they are pain-free.

## Knee pain

- Often aggravated by: deep squats, lunges, jumping, running downhill, kneeling.
- Friendlier options: box squats to a higher box, partial-range goblet squats, reverse lunges (usually easier than forward lunges), step-ups to a low step, glute bridges, hip thrusts, hamstring curls, cycling.
- Cue: keep the knee tracking over the middle of the foot and control the lowering.

## Lower-back pain

- Often aggravated by: heavy deadlifts, bent-over rows, good mornings, sit-ups, loaded twisting, long periods of bending.
- Friendlier options: chest-supported rows, goblet squats with light load, glute bridges, hip thrusts, bird dogs, dead bugs, side planks, walking.
- Cue: keep the spine neutral, brace the trunk, hinge at the hips, and avoid rounding under load. Staying gently active usually helps recovery more than bed rest.

## Shoulder pain

- Often aggravated by: overhead pressing, dips, wide-grip bench pressing, behind-the-neck movements, upright rows.
- Friendlier options: landmine or incline pressing, neutral-grip dumbbell pressing, push-ups within a comfortable range, rows, face pulls, band pull-aparts, external-rotation work.
- Cue: keep the shoulder blades controlled and avoid pain-provoking positions such as the bottom of a dip.

## Wrist and elbow pain

- Often aggravated by: push-ups on flat hands, front squats, straight-bar curls, skull crushers, heavy gripping.
- Friendlier options: push-ups on fists or handles, dumbbells with a neutral grip, cable or band exercises, lighter loads with higher reps.

## Ankle and foot problems

- Often aggravated by: running, jumping, calf raises (with Achilles tendon pain).
- Friendlier options: cycling, rowing, seated exercises; calf raises only with slow tempo and within tolerance as guided by a physiotherapist.

## Returning after an injury or a break

- Restart at about 50-70% of previous loads and volume, then build back over 2-4 weeks.
- Re-introduce impact and explosive work last.
- If an exercise repeatedly provokes symptoms, replace it rather than pushing through.
