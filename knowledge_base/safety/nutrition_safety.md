# Nutrition Safety and Disordered Eating

Source: Coachin summary of general dietetic safety guidance and public eating-disorder awareness resources. Coachin is not a dietitian or mental-health service.

## Minimum intake and rate of weight loss

- Do not recommend daily intakes below about 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 kcal for men without medical supervision; for active people the safe floor is usually higher.
- Target a weight-loss rate of about 0.5-1% of body weight per week. Faster loss increases muscle loss, fatigue, nutrient deficiency and gallstone risk.
- Very-low-calorie diets (under about 800 kcal/day) must only be used under medical supervision.
- Do not recommend calorie restriction for anyone who is underweight (BMI under 18.5), pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, or has a history of an eating disorder, unless directed by their clinician.

## Signs of possible disordered eating

The coach should gently stop weight-loss coaching and suggest professional support if the user mentions:

- Skipping meals or fasting for long periods to compensate for eating.
- Making themselves vomit, or using laxatives, diuretics or diet pills to control weight.
- Exercising to "burn off" food, or feeling intense guilt or anxiety when missing a workout.
- Fear of gaining weight despite being at a low weight, or rapid unexplained weight loss.
- Binge eating episodes with a feeling of loss of control.
- Missed periods related to low body weight or intense training.

Suggested response: acknowledge their feelings without judgement, avoid discussing numbers (calories, weights), encourage them to speak with a doctor or an eating-disorder support service in their country, and offer to focus on non-weight goals such as strength, energy and wellbeing.

## Supplements and medication

- The coach must not advise starting, stopping or changing prescription medications.
- Avoid recommending weight-loss pills, fat burners, stimulant stacks or anabolic substances.
- People with medical conditions, those who are pregnant, and those taking medication should check with a doctor or pharmacist before taking supplements.

## Food allergies and intolerances

- Always respect stated allergies and intolerances in meal suggestions.
- Severe allergies require strict avoidance; the coach should never suggest "trying a small amount".
