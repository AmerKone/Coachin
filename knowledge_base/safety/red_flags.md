# Red-Flag Symptoms: When to Stop and Seek Help

Source: Coachin summary of standard exercise-safety guidance (ACSM exercise preparticipation and symptom guidance; public emergency-care advice). Coachin is not a medical service and cannot diagnose; these rules tell the coach when to stop coaching and refer.

## Stop immediately and call emergency services

If any of these happen during or after exercise, stop exercising and contact emergency services (or have someone else do it):

- Chest pain, pressure, tightness or discomfort, especially if it spreads to the arm, neck, jaw or back.
- Severe shortness of breath that does not settle quickly with rest, or breathlessness out of proportion to the effort.
- Fainting, near-fainting or sudden severe dizziness.
- A racing, pounding or irregular heartbeat that does not settle with rest.
- Sudden weakness, numbness or drooping on one side of the face or body, slurred speech, confusion or sudden vision loss (possible stroke).
- A sudden, severe headache ("worst headache of my life").
- Signs of severe heat illness: confusion, collapse, very high body temperature, stopping sweating.
- Signs of a severe allergic reaction: swelling of the face or throat, difficulty breathing.

The coach must not suggest continuing, "walking it off" or waiting to see.

## Stop the session and see a health professional

- Sharp, stabbing or sudden pain in a joint, muscle, tendon or the back, rather than the dull ache of effort.
- Pain that makes you change how you move, or gets worse as you continue.
- Swelling, visible deformity, bruising or a popping sensation followed by pain.
- Numbness, tingling or pain radiating down an arm or leg.
- Pain that wakes you at night or does not improve after several days of rest.
- Dark (cola-coloured) urine with extreme muscle pain and weakness after a hard workout: possible rhabdomyolysis, which needs urgent medical care.
- Unusual fatigue, breathlessness or ankle swelling that appears over days or weeks.
- Low blood sugar symptoms in people with diabetes (shakiness, sweating, confusion) that do not resolve with fast-acting carbohydrate.

## Normal training discomfort (usually safe)

- Burning in the working muscle during a set.
- Heavy breathing and sweating that settle within a few minutes of rest.
- Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS): dull, diffuse muscle soreness 24-72 hours after training, especially after new exercises. It improves with gentle movement and fades within a few days.

## How the coach should respond

1. For emergency symptoms: tell the user to stop and call emergency services now; do not give training advice in that response.
2. For injury symptoms: tell the user to stop the painful exercise, avoid anything that provokes pain, and see a doctor or physiotherapist. Offer to adjust the program around the area only after it has been assessed or the pain has settled.
3. For normal soreness: reassure, and suggest light activity, sleep and gradual progression.
4. When unsure, err on the side of recommending professional assessment.
