# Energy Needs and Macronutrients

Source: Coachin summary of standard sports-nutrition practice (Mifflin-St Jeor equation; Dietary Guidelines macronutrient ranges; ISSN position stands). General guidance, not individual medical or dietetic advice.

## Estimating daily energy needs

1. Basal metabolic rate (BMR), Mifflin-St Jeor equation:
   - Men: BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age (years) + 5
   - Women: BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age (years) - 161
2. Multiply by an activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE):
   - Sedentary (little exercise): 1.2
   - Lightly active (1-3 sessions/week): 1.375
   - Moderately active (3-5 sessions/week): 1.55
   - Very active (6-7 sessions/week or physical job): 1.725
3. These are estimates with roughly +/-10% error. Track body weight trends for 2-3 weeks and adjust intake by 100-200 kcal per day if the trend does not match the goal.

## Calorie targets by goal

- Fat loss: a deficit of about 300-500 kcal per day below TDEE (or roughly 10-20%). Aim to lose about 0.5-1% of body weight per week. Faster loss increases muscle loss, hunger and fatigue.
- Muscle gain: a surplus of about 200-300 kcal per day (about 5-10% above TDEE). Aim to gain about 0.25-0.5% of body weight per month for experienced lifters, or slightly faster for beginners.
- Maintenance / general fitness / recomposition: eat around TDEE; beginners can often lose fat and gain muscle at the same time with good protein intake and training.
- Never recommend very low intakes without medical supervision (see nutrition safety guidance).

## Macronutrients

- Protein: 1.4-2.0 g per kg of body weight per day for people who train (see protein guidance). 4 kcal per gram.
- Fat: about 20-35% of calories, and not below about 0.5 g per kg per day to support hormones and absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. 9 kcal per gram.
- Carbohydrate: the remainder of calories. Fuels training, especially higher-volume and endurance work. Typical ranges are 3-5 g/kg per day for moderate training and 5-7 g/kg for high training volumes. 4 kcal per gram.
- Fibre: about 25-38 g per day from vegetables, fruit, legumes and whole grains.
- Alcohol: 7 kcal per gram with no nutritional value; it impairs recovery and sleep.

## Example calculation

A 30-year-old woman, 165 cm, 65 kg, training 4 times per week:
- BMR = 10 x 65 + 6.25 x 165 - 5 x 30 - 161 = 650 + 1031 - 150 - 161 = 1370 kcal
- TDEE = 1370 x 1.55 = about 2125 kcal
- Fat-loss target: about 1700-1800 kcal per day
- Protein: 1.6 g/kg x 65 = about 105 g; fat: about 55-60 g; carbohydrate: the remainder (about 180-200 g)

## Practical tips

- Build meals around a protein source, vegetables or fruit, a carbohydrate source, and some healthy fat.
- Consistency across weeks matters more than perfection on any single day.
- Weigh yourself under the same conditions (morning, after the bathroom) and look at weekly averages rather than daily numbers, which fluctuate with water and salt.
