# Protein, Meal Timing and Hydration

Source: Coachin summary of the ISSN position stands on protein and exercise (2017) and on nutrient timing, and general hydration guidance. General guidance, not individual medical or dietetic advice.

## How much protein

- People who strength train: 1.4-2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day.
- During a calorie deficit, the upper part of the range (about 1.8-2.2 g/kg) helps preserve muscle.
- For people with a lot of body fat, base the calculation on a goal or lean body weight rather than current weight.
- People with kidney disease should follow their doctor's protein advice; high-protein diets are not appropriate for everyone.

## Distribution across the day

- Spread protein over 3-5 meals of roughly 0.3-0.4 g/kg each (often 20-40 g per meal).
- Include a protein-rich meal or snack within a few hours before and after training; the exact "anabolic window" is wider than once believed.
- A protein-rich snack before bed (e.g. Greek yogurt, milk, cottage cheese) can contribute to daily intake.

## Good protein sources

- Animal: chicken, turkey, fish, eggs, lean beef or lamb, Greek yogurt, milk, cottage cheese, cheese.
- Plant: lentils, chickpeas, beans, tofu, tempeh, edamame, seitan, soy milk. Combining sources across the day provides all essential amino acids.
- Protein powder (whey, casein, soy, pea) is a convenient supplement but not required.

## Meal timing around training

- 1-3 hours before training: a normal meal with carbohydrate and protein.
- Less than 1 hour before: a small, easy-to-digest snack (fruit, yogurt, a slice of bread with honey) if hungry.
- After training: a meal with protein and carbohydrate within a few hours, especially after long or intense sessions or if training again within 24 hours.
- Training fasted is fine for some people; if strength or energy drops, eat beforehand.

## Hydration

- A practical guide: drink regularly through the day so urine is pale yellow.
- Before exercise: about 5-7 ml per kg of body weight in the 2-4 hours before a session.
- During exercise longer than about an hour, or in heat, drink regularly and consider electrolytes (sodium) to replace sweat losses.
- Losing more than about 2% of body weight in sweat during a session can reduce performance; weigh before and after long sessions to learn your sweat rate.
- Stop exercising and seek help for signs of heat illness: dizziness, confusion, nausea, headache, stopping sweating, or very high body temperature.

## Supplements with good evidence

- Creatine monohydrate: 3-5 g per day improves strength and power for most people. People with kidney disease should consult a doctor first.
- Caffeine: about 3 mg/kg 30-60 minutes before training can improve performance; avoid late in the day and in people sensitive to it or with heart rhythm problems or high blood pressure.
- Vitamin D: only if deficient, as advised by a doctor.
Supplements are optional; food, sleep and training matter far more.
