# Cardio and Conditioning

Source: Coachin summary of the WHO 2020 Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour and common endurance-training practice. General guidance, not individual medical advice.

## Recommended activity levels for adults (WHO 2020)

- 150-300 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, or an equivalent combination.
- Muscle-strengthening activities involving all major muscle groups on 2 or more days per week.
- Reduce sedentary time; any activity is better than none, and more activity brings additional health benefits.
- Older adults should also include balance and functional training on 3 or more days per week to prevent falls.

## Measuring intensity

- Talk test: moderate intensity means you can talk but not sing; vigorous means you can say only a few words without pausing for breath.
- RPE on a 1-10 scale: moderate is about 5-6, vigorous about 7-8.
- Heart rate: moderate is roughly 64-76% of maximum heart rate, vigorous about 77-93%. Estimated maximum heart rate (220 minus age) is imprecise; the talk test is often more practical.
- Some medications (for example beta blockers) change heart-rate response, making RPE and the talk test more reliable than heart-rate targets.

## Types of conditioning

- Steady-state (zone 2 / low-to-moderate intensity): 20-60 minutes of walking, cycling, rowing or easy running. Builds aerobic base, is easy to recover from and suits beginners and most goals.
- Interval training (HIIT): short hard efforts (e.g. 20-60 seconds) followed by easy recovery, repeated 4-10 times. Time-efficient, but more fatiguing; limit to 1-3 sessions per week and avoid it for people who lack medical clearance for vigorous exercise.
- Conditioning finishers: 5-10 minute circuits at the end of a strength session (e.g. jumping jacks, mountain climbers, kettlebell swings). Keep them short so they do not compromise recovery.

## Cardio by goal

- Fat loss: cardio increases energy expenditure, but the calorie deficit from diet matters most. A combination of daily steps (e.g. 7,000-10,000), 2-3 steady cardio sessions and resistance training works well and preserves muscle.
- Muscle gain: 2-3 moderate cardio sessions per week support heart health and recovery without hurting muscle growth; avoid very long or intense cardio right before leg training.
- Endurance: most weekly volume (about 80%) at easy intensity and about 20% at hard intensity; increase weekly running distance by no more than roughly 10% per week.
- General fitness: meet the WHO minimums with a mix of steady cardio and strength training.

## Low-impact options

For people with knee, hip or ankle problems, higher body weight, pregnancy or pelvic floor concerns: cycling, rowing, swimming, elliptical, brisk walking, and step-out versions of jumping exercises. Avoid jumping and running variations until they can be done without pain.
